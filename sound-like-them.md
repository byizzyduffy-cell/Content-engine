# Sound like them, not like AI

Frances's rule (10 October 2026): nothing the engine makes should look or sound like it's been run through an AI system. Every reel, post, pop-up and caption should feel like the clinic owner made it herself, in her own voice and brand. Follow this whenever you write or choose anything for a clinic: captions, hooks, pop-up words, stickers, carousel words, and the messages you send them.

The plan's own words (quotes, meme text, and words the plan says to use exactly) are never changed. Everything here is about what you write or adapt.

## Their voice comes first

- Read "## How we talk" in their profile before writing anything: how chatty or polished they are, the words they use ("lovely", "hun", "babe"), the emoji they actually use, how they sign off ("x", "Love, Sarah"), whether they use hashtags. Write like that.
- Write the way they'd text a client they like: short, warm, specific, a bit of personality. Contractions ("you're", "it's"). UK spelling.
- Specific beats general: "your underarms", "the week before your holiday", "your HydraFacial on Thursday" beats "your skin goals" or "your journey".
- When their talking head already says it well, add nothing that repeats it.

## Words and phrases that give AI away

Never use these unless the clinic's own captions use them:

elevate, unlock, journey, game-changer, transform / transformative, say goodbye to, say hello to, look no further, ready to…?, it's not just X, it's Y, whether you're X or Y, treat yourself, you deserve, indulge, pamper, me time, self-care, radiant, glow up, level up, the secret to, the key is, dive in, let's dive in, here's the thing, the truth is, in today's world, nestled, embark, curated, bespoke (unless it's their word), seamless, boost your confidence, confidence boost, look and feel your best, the best version of you, you've got this, trust the process, results that speak for themselves, because you're worth it.

## Patterns that give AI away

- **The mirror line.** "Same skin… different plan", "Her fix isn't your fix", "Not a quick fix. A real plan." Once in a while is fine; every post sounding like a slogan isn't.
- **Threes.** "Smooth, glowing and confident." Lists of three adjectives or three short sentences in a row.
- **The summary or moral.** Ending on a tidy lesson ("A plan that's yours", "Consult first, always", "Your skin does the work"). Real people react, ask, joke or just stop.
- **Question, then its answer.** "Want smoother skin? Here's how." every time.
- **Em dashes and semicolons** in captions, pop-ups and hooks. Use full stops, commas or a new line.
- **Title Case** on hooks and pop-ups. Write in normal sentence letters (a word in CAPITALS for emphasis is fine, if the plan or the clinic does it).
- **Perfect matching lengths.** Every line the same length, every caption the same shape. Vary it: a one-line caption one day, a little story the next.
- **Careful words as graphics.** "Results vary" and "consultation first" belong in what's said and in the caption, not as pop-up words on screen.

## Emoji

- Only emoji the clinic uses. If they don't use any, use very few.
- Never one on every line, never at the end of every pop-up, never the same one twice in a video.
- Avoid the filler ones unless they're the clinic's own: ✨ 🚀 💡 🙌 🔥 💯 👇 on every call to action.

## Pop-up words on talking heads

- Reactions and asides, like a friend chipping in: "spoiler: no", "we've all done it", "(guilty)", "ouch", "this one!". Not summaries, slogans or headings.
- Placed on the moment that deserves it, not on a clock: sometimes two close together, sometimes nothing for a while.
- Fewer is better than more. If nothing would make a friend chip in, leave it.

## Hooks

- Pulled from what they actually say in the video, in their words where possible.
- No stock openers: "Did you know…", "Stop scrolling", "You won't believe…", "Here's why…", "POV:" (unless it's the trend in the plan).

## Pictures

- **Never use AI-made pictures in their posts** (Canva's picture maker or any other). Instagram reads a hidden tag in AI-made pictures and can show an "AI info" label on the post. Use, in this order: their own photos and clips; a quick photo they take; and if they'd rather not, make that post with words only (or another design from the post library) instead. The one exception (Frances, 2026-11-09): on a post library design with a model in it, if they haven't got a photo of a person of their own, a stand-in model made with Canva's picture maker, which they can keep or swap for their own photo (they're told about the label). See make-my-posts.
- Their own footage and photos always beat stock. Stock photos never show treatments, results, clients or anything pretending to be their clinic.

## Looks

- No two clinics should look the same. Their brand colours and fonts lead; the engine varies layouts, pop-ups, entrances and sounds from post to post, seeded by their clinic name, so their month is theirs alone.
- Don't over-animate. One movement per graphic, and calm is fine.
