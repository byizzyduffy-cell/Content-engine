# Writing about each treatment: the careful-wording rules

Frances's rules for anything written about a treatment: posts, reel words, carousel slides, talking points, hooks, pop-ups and captions. They come from UK advertising guidance (the ASA and CAP, who police ads, Instagram included) and UK law. Plan-my-month uses them most when it rewrites a skin or aesthetics post around the treatment a clinic is promoting this month.

What a clinic owner says in their own talking head is their choice (Frances's rule): only flag a line the treatment can't deliver, and never cut it without asking. These rules are for everything Claude writes.

This isn't legal advice. Where something's uncertain, keep to the safest wording.

## Every treatment

- **Claims need proof.** Anything a client would take as a fact needs evidence. So never: "safe", "pain-free", "painless", "damage-free", "guaranteed", "permanent", "forever", "instant results".
- **Results vary**, and suitability comes first: a consultation (and a patch test where it applies) before treatment.
- **Never invent anything:** results, numbers of sessions, how long results last, prices, reviews, before and afters, statistics.
- **Urgency must be true.** "3 spaces left" only when there really are 3.
- **Never aimed at under-18s** for cosmetic treatments (laser, peels, microneedling, injectables, PMU, body contouring): no prom, school-leaver or teen themes.
- **Never trivial or rushed:** no "lunchtime fix", "hurry, ends midnight", countdown pressure or "book before it's gone". People must have time to think it over.
- **Never the price as the reason to book**, and **never play on insecurities** ("fix your flaws", "hate your…?"). Lead with what the client wants.
- **A clinic's own offer** (their promotions this month) can go in, exactly as they gave it: their price, terms and true end date, word for word, as the reason to message now. The treatment and the consultation stay the main message; never "hurry", "last chance", a countdown or an invented number of spaces.

## Laser hair removal (and IPL)

- **Hair reduction**, never "permanent", "hair free for life", "completely hair free" or "forever".
- It's a **course**, because hair grows in cycles. Results vary with skin and hair type. Patch test first.
- Talk about what to expect, never "painless". "Changes lives" is fine.

## Skin (facials, peels, microneedling, resurfacing, dermaplaning, LED)

- **Keep to how the skin looks and feels:** "smoother-looking", "brighter-looking", "fresher-looking". Never "anti-ageing", "boosts collagen", "detox" or "cell regeneration".
- **No acne claims:** never treat, cure or clear acne, and take care with "breakouts", "spots" and "blemishes". Talk about how the skin looks and feels and the consultation. **LED:** no acne claims at all.
- **Peels and resurfacing:** downtime and suitability depend on the treatment and the skin, so never "no downtime" unless that's true of theirs. Say which kind of resurfacing it is. Aftercare and sun protection matter: say so where it fits.
- **Microneedling:** usually a course; results build and vary.
- **Prescription skincare** (tretinoin and the like) is prescription-only: never promote it.

## Aesthetics (polynucleotides, fillers, skin boosters, consultations)

- **Anti-wrinkle injections can't be advertised to the public**, even by a nurse or doctor. Never: "Botox", "Beautox", "tox", any brand name, "wrinkle-relaxing injections", "injections for sweating", or fillers and anti-wrinkle injections in the same post. What you can promote is **a consultation about lines and wrinkles**, the practitioner's expertise and the clinic.
- **Fillers and skin boosters** can be promoted with the cosmetic rules above. Giving them to under-18s is a crime in England: never aim anything at under-18s.
- **Polynucleotides:** a course; results build gradually over weeks and vary. Never promise they "regenerate", "repair" or "boost collagen": talk about how the skin looks and feels, and the consultation.
- **Other prescription-only treatments** (vitamin B12 injections, weight-loss injections): never.

## Body contouring

- No weight-loss or inch-loss claims: no "lose inches", "drop a dress size", "melt fat", "% fat loss" or "permanent fat reduction". It isn't a weight-loss treatment.
- Talk about how it works, a course, realistic expectations, results that vary, and a consultation first. Never "20-minute" or "quick fix". Never shame bodies.
- **Wood therapy and lymphatic drainage** are massages: talk about how they feel, the pressure, comfort and the appointment. Never "detox", "flush out toxins", "drains fat", "removes cellulite" or medical claims about swelling. New or unexplained swelling goes to a healthcare professional first, and after surgery the surgical team's advice comes first (the Starter Plan's body posts say so).

## Semi-permanent make-up

- It counts as tattooing: never for under-18s, even with a parent's consent.
- "Semi-permanent": it fades over time and differs with skin type; a top-up is usually part of it. Never promise how long it lasts. Patch test first. Never "pain-free", and never name prescription numbing creams.

## Tattoo removal

- Never "complete removal", "100% removed", "gone for good", "erase", "scar-free" or "painless". Say fading, lightening, or removal over a course of sessions, and that results vary with the ink, the skin and the tattoo's age.
- Patch test and consultation first. Fading for a cover-up is a good angle.

## Lash and brow

- A patch test before tints, lifts and lamination ("before your first appointment", not a set number of hours).
- No "damage-free" or "won't harm your natural lashes". No growth claims for serums.

## Hair

- **Colour:** an allergy alert (skin) test before colour, as the colour's maker says: never suggest skipping it. Never promise a colour result in one appointment ("platinum in one visit"): it depends on the hair's history, and lightening dark or coloured hair can take more than one. A consultation first.
- **Treatments** (bond builders, keratin, glossing): talk about how hair looks and feels ("smoother", "shinier-looking", "easier to style"). Never "repairs damage", "permanent straightening", "fixes breakage", "stops hair loss" or "makes your hair grow".
- **Extensions:** never "no damage" or "won't harm your own hair"; how long they last depends on the hair and the aftercare.

## Nails

- Never "won't damage your natural nails", "strengthens", "makes your nails grow", "hypoallergenic" or "allergy-free". Gels and acrylics can cause allergies in some people: a consultation, and the salon's own patch test if they do one.
- How long a set lasts depends on the nails and how they're looked after: never a promised number of weeks.
- Nails, cuticles or skin that look infected or sore go to a GP or pharmacist, never "we'll treat it".

## Beauty (facials, waxing, threading, massage, spray tans, make-up)

- **Facials:** the Skin rules above.
- **Waxing and threading:** never "pain-free" or "permanent". A quick skin check before a first wax (and a patch test where the salon does one); some skincare and medication can make waxing unsuitable, which the consultation covers.
- **Massage:** talk about how it feels and the appointment. Never health or medical claims ("detox", "cures", "heals", "boosts your immune system").
- **Spray tans:** a patch test before a first tan. A fake tan is never sun protection: never "safe in the sun" or "protects your skin".

## Teeth whitening

- Results vary with the teeth: never a promised number of shades, "permanent", "safe", "pain-free" or "no sensitivity". A consultation first.
- Write only what the clinic tells you it offers, in its own words, and never name peroxide strengths or products.
